Current established canon — latest decisions

Canon additions — Spiritus, contracts, and the Wyrm [EX][SLA]

Editorial status: Additive updates from the November 2026 lore discussion. Existing definitions and historical source text are preserved. Established clarifications are separated from developing hypotheses.

Established clarification: Sovereignty and Sanctum

Additional Clarification: Higher Sovereignty makes a person's Sanctum more difficult to lower through manipulation, deception, or coercion. Even when the Sanctum is lowered, elevated Sovereignty continues to resist Siphoning.

This does not make the Sanctum impossible to lower voluntarily. Spiritus retains its exact definition: “The innate ability to strengthen one’s own Sovereignty.” Spiritus does not directly strengthen Sanctum. A Steward generally must induce a lowered Sanctum to gain access for Siphoning; strong Sovereignty makes that manipulation harder and continues resisting extraction afterward.

Established clarification: nonphysical Binding Contracts

A Binding Contract attaches to a soul through authorized consent and access to Sovereignty. Interaction with a living, physical person does not make the resulting soul contract a physical manifestation. The Wyrm can maintain many nonphysical Binding Contracts simultaneously while remaining limited to one physical manifestation at a time. The Codex is the current physical manifestation. Authorized access is not a forced breach of Sanctum, nor does a contract grant unlimited control over the signatory.

Established distinction: Transmogrification and manifestation

Steward Soulcraft can Transmogrify an existing soul into a physical object. This is distinct from the Wyrm manifesting himself physically as the Codex. Neither process establishes that souls are inherently physical; their underlying nature remains unresolved.

Established distinction: prediction versus control

The Wyrm can analyze possible futures and make influential choices, but cannot guarantee that a predicted outcome will occur. He can introduce knowledge, opportunities, contracts, or a permitted manifestation; subsequent events remain beyond his direct control. The Stewards, by contrast, deliberately manipulate people, civilizations, and political circumstances to engineer preferred outcomes. Neither party is guaranteed success. The Wyrm's interventions can influence reality without amounting to direct control over the resulting history.

Developing lore: why the Wyrm introduced Spiritus [EX][SLA] — proposed, not yet confirmed

The Wyrm may have taught Spiritus to an early Ancient King after predicting that greater Sovereignty could support stronger voluntary Binding Contracts. He could foresee this as a possibility but could not confirm it without observing a civilization cultivate Spiritus over generations. If confirmed, the Kingdom's development—and potentially the Bloodline Testament—would provide the real-world result of that experiment. Stronger contractual capacity would belong to the consenting soul; it would not grant the Wyrm arbitrary new powers.

Developing lore: the Stewards' missed opportunity [EX][SLA] — hypothesis, not yet confirmed

Highly developed Sovereignty made the Ancient Kingdom's souls difficult to access and Siphon. Yet, if such souls could be exploited, they might yield greater sustenance or enable stronger, more stable, or more complex Transmogrified constructs. The Stewards' destruction of the Kingdom could therefore have eliminated an exceptionally valuable resource while removing a perceived threat. These possible benefits are untested, not established Soulcraft mechanics. The Stewards' ultimate endgame and their first discovery of the Kingdom's resistance remain open for PDN's development.

Continuity boundaries

The Wyrm's original purpose, what “the game” means, the nature of souls, the precise limits of Transmogrified consciousness, and whether the Wyrm anticipated the Codex catastrophe remain unresolved. This update does not establish the cause of Oathbreaker's lost contract memory or how his Codex contract persisted through death and Restoration. No new motive or ultimate objective is assigned to the Queen or the Stewards.

Soul principles

Sanctum protects a soul's Sovereignty (its authority over itself). Spiritus is the innate ability to strengthen one's own Sovereignty. Strong Spiritus increases resistance to Siphoning and manipulation, including when Sanctum is lowered, but is not immunity and does not strengthen the Sanctum itself.

Inscripture incorporates a soul's intact consciousness into the Library for the Wyrm's knowledge collection. Inscribed souls retain identity, memories, consciousness, Sanctum and Sovereignty. They remain conscious, can interact with other souls in their respective collections, and can refuse requests. They cannot leave their collections under their own power or be Restored. Their experience resembles a shared afterlife, though their confinement remains real. No forced dormancy is canon.

Preservation is placed by the Queen on a living being and takes precedence over Inscripture at death; it allows Restoration. Preservation and Inscripture are different powers, not a strength ranking.

Soulcraft is the Stewards' innate capacity to extract, manipulate, reshape and consume souls. Siphoning extracts spiritual essence; Transmogrification can turn souls into physical objects; Consumption uses souls as sustenance, with complete Consumption permanently destroying a soul. Exact mechanics and limits of partial Consumption and Transmogrified consciousness remain open. Elite Stewards may forcibly breach Sanctums; ordinary Stewards rely on lowered Sanctums.

The natural fate of souls not Inscribed, Preserved, or Transmogrified is unknown. Consumption is a distinct destructive outcome.

The Wyrm and its bargains

The Wyrm is an Ancient Anomaly with a vast Library of knowledge. It occasionally offers civilizations, groups or bloodlines a continuing communal afterlife through Inscripture in exchange for the knowledge of their members. It acquires knowledge passively; not all knowledge is equally valuable, and it does not pursue only exceptional knowledge. It is indifferent to a civilization's survival. The Wyrm's deeper motives and the game remain unexplained.

The Wyrm introduced Spiritus to an early Ancient King from the Library's many possible offerings. Its accumulated knowledge allows it to analyze those it meets and anticipate desires and outcomes; the precise reason Spiritus was chosen remains unknown. The Kingdom cultivated Spiritus for generations and developed a largely peaceful, contemplative, monk-like culture with strong Sovereignty.

The Bloodline Testament is the Ancient King's agreement with the Wyrm, pledging his descendants to Inscripture upon death. It is an agreement, not a mortal spell compelling the Wyrm. The abandoned idea that every descendant must personally pair Spiritus with the Wyrm to finalize it is not canon.

The Library does not reveal what it is. It reveals what each visitor is capable of perceiving. Books and pages are the Oathbreaker's subjective perception; souls are not literally turned into pages. Only the Stewards' race has the established capacity to make physical objects out of souls.

Historical arc and Accord

Rift Convergence begins → Ancient Kingdom is destroyed → Accord is established.

The Stewards detected the Ancient Kingdom's Spiritus as a threat to their soul-exploitation system. They did not manipulate or redirect Rifts themselves. Instead, they manipulated the kingdoms and civilizations entering through the Rifts, exploited the chaos of first contact, falsely blamed the Ancient Kingdom, and directed the resulting hostility toward it. The Stewards orchestrated the Kingdom's destruction through these civilizations rather than attacking it directly. The first Rift members subsequently founded the Accord upon the Kingdom's ruins. The precise identities of participating civilizations and their individual grievances remain to be developed. The Stewards were unaware of the Wyrm. The Wyrm did not care that the Kingdom fell.

The broader Core Source premise remains: the Accord later facilitated interworld exchange; its leaders were compromised by Stewards, who engineered war and dependency. The Queen's soulless insectoid colony and the Champions remain important.

Oathbreaker origin

The Oathbreaker's father incurred a crushing debt to an influential broker, who held the knight's hometown hostage. The exact confrontation remains open.

The Queen sent her biologically connected ants through Rifts and saw the Oathbreaker through one of her ants. She selected him for reasons belonging to her own game; her motive remains private and unexplained. She is not omniscient or omnipotent: her distant perception depends on her insects. The date of this observation is not fixed. She placed Preservation on the Oathbreaker while he was alive, before their first meeting. The exact method by which she applied Preservation at a distance is not yet defined. During its ancient slumber, the Wyrm sensed the Queen's influence subverting the game and manifested the Codex. Its manifestation caused a town-sized outward explosion followed by violent inward collapse. The Oathbreaker died in the catastrophe, but the Queen Restored him; he met the Queen after Restoration.

The townspeople died and were not Inscribed into the Library or Ancestral Domain. Their souls' fate is unknown. The Oathbreaker's broken oath is his failure to protect them, not intentional wrongdoing or reckless use of the Codex.

The Oathbreaker unknowingly descends from an illegitimate surviving branch of the Ancient Kingdom's royal line. His Codex is a manifestation of the Wyrm and conduit to the Ancestral Domain, not a container for souls. Through Domain Invocation, he requests a voluntary Ancestral Blessing; Inscribed ancestors retain Sovereignty and may refuse. The Codex is a signature weapon outside the normal deck.

Reserved narrative mysteries

The game; the Wyrm's ultimate purpose; the Queen's motive for Preserving Oathbreaker; the Library's objective form; and the natural fate of ordinary souls remain intentionally unknown.
