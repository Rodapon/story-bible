Story

The overarching story and how it fits the game. This document keeps the original layout and separates three kinds of content:

  • Decided: established in the original story or explicitly directed in later discussions.
  • Proposal: an idea under consideration, not yet agreed.
  • Open questions: unresolved decisions listed at the end.

Names are working names. Coalition is the current working name for the original villain placeholder X. The Accord is the interworld alliance, not the Coalition.

Premise

Decades ago, Rifts opened across different worlds. Every known Rift leads to Earth, the ordinary human world. From Earth, travelers can enter the other connected worlds, but those worlds have no known direct Rift connections to each other.

People, creatures, technology, and magic poured into Earth. Humanity was overwhelmed, but over time humans and newcomers built a shared society. The worlds formed the Accord to maintain peace and exchange resources. Eventually, tensions became an all-out war.

The war was engineered. A small ruling group from one of the Accord's founding worlds—the Coalition—secretly controls the Accord's leaders. Its members want civilizations to suffer enough that people willingly place their Faith in the Coalition and accept its rule.

A new Rift then reveals a primitive insect-like colony that the Coalition cannot control through its usual methods. The Accord calls a truce to fight the colony. Heroes from the warring worlds encounter its Queen, who saves them and sends them against the Coalition.

These heroes become Champions. Their mission is to preserve peace without closing the Rifts: they enter connected worlds to stop threats at their source or deal with dangers already spilling into Earth.

The Rifts and Earth

  • One destination: every known non-Earth Rift leads to Earth.
  • One crossroads: Earth is the gateway to all other connected worlds.
  • Shared home: generations of humans and newcomers now live on Earth.
  • Shared danger: a crisis in any world can spread through its Rift to Earth.
  • Champions' approach: solve problems inside the worlds rather than routinely sealing their connections.

Open: why Earth is the convergence point, whether Rifts can be closed, and how passage is governed. The Rifts are not established as the source of Faith.

Faith

Faith is a fundamental cosmic force. It reflects how much beings genuinely believe in, trust, or devote themselves to someone or something. The Coalition is exceptionally influential because so many beings believe in its members and their authority.

Faith is not the same as Rift-derived power. It does not determine what supernatural ability a being possesses or automatically increase the output of that ability. In the Coalition's case, Faith primarily determines how much influence it can exert over believers.

Rules of Coalition influence

  1. Coalition members can forcibly control only a few unwilling people; these include the Accord's leaders.
  2. The less Faith a soul has in them, the less they can influence it through Faith.
  3. They can influence any number of souls that place sufficient Faith in them.
  4. They cannot force someone to genuinely believe more.
  5. At sufficiently high Faith, they can alter a soul and transform its body.

What Faith gives them

  • Faith gives strength: it sustains their health and physical strength, making them extremely difficult to assassinate.
  • Souls give lifespan: they consume the souls of the faithful to stay alive. A consumed soul is gone.

Open: whether everyone can harness Faith, what counts as genuine belief, and whether Faith manifests differently in different beings. The Queen and Coalition are not established as embodiments of opposing cosmic forces.

The Coalition

The Coalition is a small group of individuals ruling its own world. They are the same race as their people but possess unusual, witch-like powers.

Why they need the war

The Coalition consumes souls faster than its home world can replace them through births. It needs believers from other worlds to survive. It cannot force whole populations to develop genuine Faith, so it uses the Accord's controlled leaders to manufacture fear, conflict, and desperation. Then it presents itself as the solution.

Earth's central position lets the Coalition influence many worlds through shared institutions without conquering each one directly.

Their world

From outside, the Coalition's world looks like a utopia. Its people live without obvious conflict. But the bodies of consumed followers decompose into a remarkable building material: every building on the Coalition's world is made from dead followers.

Keeping the secret

The Coalition controls what faithful followers and compromised Accord leaders say. Propaganda and selective truth keep its methods hidden. Its public promises of peace conceal its need for an ever-growing supply of souls.

The Queen and her world

The newly discovered world is a primitive, ant-colony-like civilization. It is separate from the AI world.

  • Subjects: the insect-like subjects have no souls, so the Coalition's usual soul-based influence cannot reach them.
  • Queen: she has a soul but has never directly encountered the Coalition.
  • Consumption: she can consume someone, retain their soul, and give birth to them again.
  • Memories: she keeps the memories of those she consumes.
  • Sensing: she can sense faithful souls being consumed by the Coalition in real time.
  • Advancement: her colony develops over time, reflecting player unlocks and achievements.

Queen and Coalition

Both use souls, but they do different things with them:

Queen Coalition
Holds souls and can rebirth people Consumes souls permanently
Preserves memories Uses souls to extend its lifespan
Commands a colony of soulless subjects Gains influence through believers' Faith

Neither side is automatically good. Preserving a soul without consent can still be a form of control. The Queen's deeper motives and the origin of her abilities remain open.

Souls and rebirth

  • Souls can be held, influenced, altered, or consumed under particular conditions.
  • Coalition consumption destroys a soul permanently.
  • The Queen retains souls and can rebirth their owners.
  • The Champions' souls are linked together.
  • While close together, Champions can recover from near death.
  • Going down weakens their connection.
  • When everyone goes down, the party loses its connection in the field and returns to the Queen's world through rebirth. A full wipe ends the run.

Important: the exact process of rebirth, whether Champions consented, and whether they can reclaim their souls are not decided. Earth is the Rift network's crossroads; the Queen's colony is the resurrection and progression hub.

The Champions

The Champions come from worlds that were recently at war. They are not disposable consumables. They cross the Rifts to preserve coexistence, including by:

  • Stopping conflicts before they reach Earth.
  • Tracing threats back to their source worlds.
  • Stabilizing dangerous magical, political, or technological crises.
  • Investigating the Coalition's manipulation and propaganda.

Not every problem comes from the Coalition. Each civilization has conflicts of its own.

The Oathbreaker

The Oathbreaker is a fallen warrior trying to prove his oath was never truly broken. His Codex holds souls that do not willingly serve him; he compels them to aid his pursuit of atonement.

Proposal: his struggle mirrors the Queen and Coalition on a personal scale: each believes it has a reason to control souls. His home world, role in the war, and ultimate fate are not decided.

How it fits the game

  • Runs: independent roguelike runs; the wider story unfolds across many runs.
  • The map: choosing a world means entering its Rift from Earth. Worlds have their own terrain, generation, and scenarios.
  • Queen's world: persistent hub showing unlocks and achievements.
  • Linked souls: explain downed recovery and the party-wipe rebirth loop.
  • War news: brief Accord broadcasts provide the official account, sometimes misleadingly.
  • The ending: not decided.

Proposals

None of the following are agreed yet.

Story

  • The truce is the first clue: every leader agrees unusually quickly after years of war. The Coalition brokers it and takes credit, increasing Faith.
  • The propaganda is true: the Queen eats people. The Champions eventually learn she ate them too.
  • Why the Champions matter: souls held by the Queen may be harder for the Coalition to claim, while ordinary armies can be turned.
  • How she learned the truth: she consumed someone who knew the Coalition's secret.
  • Her progress has a cost: each advance in her colony draws on knowledge from someone she consumed.
  • The Queen's motivation: she opposes the permanent disappearance of souls she can sense, while also protecting her colony.
  • Coalition ideology: its members justify soul consumption as necessary to preserve civilization.

Coalition's world

  • Joining the city: citizens treat becoming building material as an honor.
  • Empty districts: the population declines while the beautiful city grows.
  • Exported stone: other worlds unknowingly buy material made from dead followers.
  • Missing assassins: records reveal failed attempts to uncover the truth.

Gameplay

  • Faith stages: trust permits subtle influence, devotion permits commands, and absolute Faith permits soul transformation. Exact thresholds are not decided.
  • Faith pressure: Coalition influence grows in worlds the player skips; this affects control and encounters, not the intrinsic output of Rift-derived powers.
  • World as an act: each world bundles map generation, biomes, scenarios, enemies, and card affinities, potentially supporting mods.
  • Coalition's world as final act: the final enemy's vulnerability reflects how much Coalition influence the Champions disrupted.
  • Transformed faithful: devoted followers become unusual enemies.
  • Narrative discovery: across runs, players discover the manufactured war, the Queen's methods, and the Coalition's utopia.
