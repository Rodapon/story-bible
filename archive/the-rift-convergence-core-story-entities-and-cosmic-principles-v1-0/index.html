THE RIFT CONVERGENCE

Core Story, Ancient Entities & Cosmic Principles

World Codex — v1.0

Scope: A standalone reference containing only the overarching story, ancient entities, and cosmic/spiritual principles. Character biography, combat systems, card mechanics, and detailed Oathbreaker progression are intentionally excluded.

Canon priority: The latest Faith retcon governs this document: only X / the Coalition harnesses Faith. Will is a distinct, inward source of power, not a form of Faith; neither the Queen nor the Archivist draws power from Faith.

Status: Established means accepted canon; Working means not yet finalized; Open means unresolved.

I. CORE STORY

The Rift Convergence

Long before the modern era, civilizations existed in isolation across separate worlds. Decades before the game's present, interdimensional Rifts appeared, with Earth serving as the crossroads of the modern network: known modern Rifts connect other worlds to Earth rather than directly to one another.

Contact brought exchange of cultures, technologies, resources, and magic, but also rivalry and conflict. The interworld Accord was formed to preserve coexistence. Its member civilizations eventually became entangled in wars, and key Accord leaders fell under secret Coalition manipulation.

The Rifts are passages, not the source of Faith or Will. The heroes' larger task is to address threats within connected worlds, not indiscriminately seal every Rift.

X and the manufactured salvation

X / the Coalition is a small group of ancient, powerful beings associated with a founding Rift world. The Coalition engineers instability, suffering, and war, then offers rescue so frightened populations voluntarily place genuine Faith in its members.

  • Faith amplifies the Coalition's existing supernatural capacities; it cannot be manufactured by simply ordering someone to believe.
  • Soul Consumption destroys individual souls irreversibly and sustains the Coalition's longevity.
  • Its apparent utopia is constructed from the remains of deceased followers, masking exploitation beneath promises of salvation.
  • X condemns Will as dangerous, impure, or selfish because self-directed power undermines dependence on external saviors.

The ancient kingdom and the eradication of Will

An ancient human kingdom existed centuries or millennia before the modern Convergence. Through an exceptional, small precursor Rift [Working], its early ruler encountered the Archivist in the form of an old man, who revealed the existence of Will through teaching rather than creating it.

Generations of scholars studied Will, souls, and royal spiritual inheritance, developing Ancestral Dominion. Their knowledge threatened X's ideology: individuals could become stronger through their own conviction instead of surrendering belief to the Coalition.

During the first chaos of the modern Rifts, X launched an intentional genocide against the kingdom and those who practiced, taught, or protected Will. The attack preceded the Sovereign Veil and was not caused by it.

The Sovereign Veil: the last contingency

Only in the kingdom's final hours did the reigning king, his family, and surviving scholars attempt the Sovereign Veil: an unfinished, untested spiritual construct layered over the Archivist's innate Retention.

Design element Purpose and effect
Concealment Hide the Archivist, the Ancestral Domain, and their spiritual connections from X.
Bloodline-only future Retention Reduce the detectable activity of soul collection by limiting future eligible transfers to the designated royal bloodline.
Restricted inheritance access Prevent royal heirs from freely reaching the Archivist's unrelated, pre-Veil collection.
Ancestral transfer Route eligible bloodline souls into Retention at death, unless a prior incompatible Preservation Condition takes precedence.
Unintended concealment The Veil also hides the Domain from the Queen, although she was not its intended target.
  • The emergency initially retained souls of the dying kingdom, including some people outside the royal bloodline; later collection was bloodline-restricted.
  • The Veil did not injure the Archivist, erase his original archive, or supply him with a new innate power.
  • The construct lacked tested redundancy, failover, and a completed reversal method.

The Queen's colony and the Champions

A Rift reveals an unusual insectoid colony whose ordinary subjects are soulless. Their nature resists the Coalition's conventional soul-targeting influence, prompting the Accord to declare a truce against the colony.

The Queen preserves selected living individuals for reasons tied to her undisclosed ambitions. When preserved Champions die, she can Restore them, creating the narrative basis for repeated expeditions and returns to the colony.

Her selections are opportunistic rather than proof of moral approval. She can observe distant places through her colony but cannot see through the Sovereign Veil or recover souls already retained by the Archivist.

The collision of ancient conditions

During the Oathbreaker's hometown crisis, the Queen establishes Preservation on his soul while he is still alive. That condition takes precedence over the Sovereign Veil's pending death-triggered Retention claim.

The Archivist responds to the threatened claim by manifesting as The Codex, a physical conduit that cannot act without a wielder. The knight's own uncontrolled use of the newly accessible inherited power causes the catastrophic breach; the precise mechanism remains Open.

This incident connects the Queen's preservation, the Archivist's retention, the concealed royal inheritance, and the ongoing conflict surrounding X without making either ancient entity directly responsible for the knight's choices.

II. ANCIENT ENTITIES

X / The Coalition

Attribute Canon
Nature A small group of ancient beings, with X as its central antagonist or ideological force.
Power source Faith received through genuine belief from followers; exclusively harnessed by this group.
Longevity Consumption of souls, which permanently destroys spiritual continuity.
Method Orchestrates crises and offers salvation to cultivate dependence and belief.
Ideology Condemns Will because self-reliance threatens the Coalition's power structure.
Unresolved Membership, exact origins, ultimate plans, and relative strength versus the Queen.

The Queen

An ancient, abstract insectoid matriarch whose powers are innate, not derived from Faith. Her authority spans the creation of life, the Preservation of living souls, and the Restoration of souls she has already preserved.

Ability Rule
Life creation Creates soulless insectoid subjects; she is not established as a creator of souls from nothing.
Preservation Establishes a restorable condition while the subject is alive.
Restoration Returns a previously preserved soul to embodied life after death.
Collective Sight — The Thousand Eyes Observes through biologically linked subjects that scout, forage, and survive like members of an ant colony.
Passive awareness Receives basic impressions of movement, danger, and unusual events across her colony.
Focused sight Directs attention through an individual subject's available senses.
Collective observation Combines scouting across many subjects to search for useful potential Champions.

Limits: The Queen is not omniscient; her scouts must physically reach places and cannot inherently read hidden souls or penetrate the Veil. She cannot retroactively preserve or restore an already-retained soul, and her subjects do not supply Faith.

The Archivist

An incomprehensibly ancient entity whose innate domain is Retention. It collects real, conscious souls without preserving their ability to return to life.

Aspect Canon
The Library Its original, pre-Veil collection of knowledgeable souls gathered across civilizations.
Retention Stores individual identities, memories, and consciousness indefinitely without restorability.
Old-man manifestation Influences the physical world indirectly through knowledge, counsel, and direction.
The Codex manifestation A physical conduit requiring another individual to wield it.
The Ancestral Domain The kingdom's concealed spiritual inheritance under the Veil's restrictions.
Veil limitation Future Retention is bloodline-restricted; the original Library remains intact but inaccessible to the royal inheritor.

Limits: The Archivist cannot independently wage physical campaigns, wield its own sword, or Restore retained souls. The Veil is an imposed conditional overlay, not damage to its essential Retention power.

III. COSMIC PRINCIPLES & SPIRITUAL LAWS

Foundational concepts

Principle Definition Boundary
Faith Genuine belief from followers that X / the Coalition alone harnesses to amplify existing supernatural powers. Cannot be forced into authenticity; does not automatically create new innate abilities.
Will Independent, inward conviction that strengthens one's own soul and its existing capabilities. Not inward Faith under the latest retcon; does not automatically grant soul command or resurrection.
Soul The real spiritual identity and consciousness of an individual. A retained soul is a person, not a recording or imitation.
Spiritual continuity The ongoing identity and, where preserved, the capacity for future embodiment. Continuity of identity does not necessarily mean restorability.
Ancestral Dominion A hereditary authority developed within the ancient royal line to command authorized retained souls. Not an automatic property of Will; does not grant access to The Library.

Four distinct soul operations

Operation Associated entity Effect on soul Identity remains? Can return to life?
Consume X / Coalition Permanently destroys spiritual identity and continuity. No No
Retain Archivist Stores the conscious soul indefinitely but without restorable continuity. Yes No
Preserve Queen Protects a living soul's capacity for future restoration. Yes Yes
Restore Queen Returns an already-preserved soul to a living body. Yes Already restored

Precedence of conditions

  1. While alive: The Queen can establish a Preservation Condition on an eligible living soul.
  2. At death: If that condition is already active, it takes precedence over the Veil's pending Retention transfer for that soul.
  3. After death: The Queen can Restore a preserved soul, but cannot retroactively Preserve or Restore a soul that has already entered Retention.
  4. After Consumption: The soul's identity and continuity are destroyed; neither Retention nor Restoration can recover it under established rules.

Governing distinctions

  • Faith and Will are distinct: The Coalition alone harnesses Faith; Will is independent personal conviction, not a lesser expression of Faith.
  • Innate authority is not interchangeable: Faith amplifies the Coalition's abilities, while the Queen's and Archivist's capacities arise from their own natures.
  • Concealment is not preservation: The Sovereign Veil hides the Domain; it does not make retained souls restorable.
  • Retention is not restoration: Souls within The Library or Ancestral Domain remain conscious but cannot be brought back by the Queen.
  • Collective Sight is not omniscience: The Queen sees through living subjects rather than automatically detecting all spiritual activity.

IV. UNRESOLVED WORLD-LEVEL QUESTIONS

  • Who founded the Coalition, and what are X's ultimate intentions beyond power and longevity?
  • How did X identify and systematically eradicate practitioners of Will?
  • Does X suspect the Archivist or concealed Ancestral Domain survived?
  • What is the Queen's long-term objective in selecting and repeatedly restoring Champions?
  • Did the Archivist willingly accept the Sovereign Veil, or was its restriction imposed against its wishes?
  • Can the Veil ever be safely reversed, given that its original designers left no completed reversal?
  • How exactly does the Queen select and establish Preservation on a distant living subject?
