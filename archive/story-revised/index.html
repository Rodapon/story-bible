Story — The Rift Convergence

Narrative design document | Revision 2 | 2026-11-05

Status convention: Established = original story material retained or a direction explicitly stated in subsequent design discussions. Working interpretation = a reconciliation needed to make those directions coexist, pending approval. Proposal = optional narrative or gameplay development. Open = not yet decided. Names remain provisional.

1. Core premise [Established, with working reconciliations]

Decades ago, Rifts began opening across otherwise isolated worlds. Every known Rift leads to Earth, the ordinary human world. In reverse, Earth is the only known gateway to all the other connected civilizations. The network is a hub-and-spoke system: the worlds are not known to have direct Rift routes to one another.

The first arrivals brought extraordinary technology, magic, creatures, religions, and people. As more civilizations crossed over, human society was overwhelmed by migration, competing interests, and the upheaval of becoming the center of a vast interworld network. Earth did not simply disappear; it changed. Generations of humans and newcomers built lives alongside one another. Trade and cooperation grew, but so did displacement, distrust, and conflict.

The connected worlds formed the Accord, a diplomatic and political agreement meant to share resources, regulate passage, and prevent catastrophe. Peace eventually fractured into a devastating war between worlds and factions. The war was not accidental. A secret ruling group from one of the Accord's founding worlds manipulated the Accord's leaders and cultivated the conditions for its own ascendancy.

That group is provisionally called the Coalition in the recent design discussion, replacing the original placeholder X. The exact organization name, membership, and public identity remain open. Its members possess abnormal powers, but their greatest advantage is their extraordinary influence over the Faith others place in them.

The Coalition engineers instability until people voluntarily look to it for safety, meaning, and leadership. It cannot simply manufacture genuine Faith by command. Its system depends on making people choose to believe.

Later, a previously unknown Rift reveals a primitive insect-like colony ruled by a Queen. The Coalition cannot influence the Queen's soulless subjects through its usual soul-based methods. The Accord abruptly declares a truce against the colony. Heroes from formerly warring worlds converge on that front, where the Queen saves them—or appears to save them—and sends them against the hidden architects of the war.

The player characters are Champions. Their larger mission is not to seal the Rifts and isolate the worlds. It is to preserve coexistence by entering the worlds and disrupting dangers at their source, including threats already emerging onto Earth. They fight to keep the network open without allowing it to become an instrument of conquest or submission.

Central thematic conflict: The Rifts made coexistence possible. Faith made mass influence possible. Who has the right to claim, command, preserve, or consume another soul?

2. The Rift network and Earth [Established direction]

The convergence rule

  1. Every known Rift in another world opens to Earth.
  2. Earth contains access points to the connected worlds.
  3. Direct Rift connections between two non-Earth worlds are not known to exist.
  4. The reason Earth is the convergence point is Open. The Coalition and Queen are not established as the creators of the Rifts.
  5. Rifts allow passage and exchange; they do not, by themselves, determine a being's Faith or the output of their powers.

Consequences

Earth is the diplomatic crossroads, trading hub, migration destination, and vulnerable center of the network. A crisis in any connected world can become an Earth crisis. Closing a Rift might stop a threat, but it might also sever families, livelihoods, supply routes, and political relationships. The Champions therefore favor intervention over permanent closure.

Working interpretation: Rift closure may be possible, but its technical feasibility, cost, and permanence remain undefined. The story does not assume that Champions can casually open or close portals.

Earth is not morally uniform

Humans can fear, welcome, exploit, or cooperate with newcomers. Nonhuman inhabitants may have been born on Earth and regard it as home. The Accord's peace is worth protecting precisely because its members do not agree on what that peace should look like.

3. Faith — a fundamental cosmic force [Established direction; mechanics partly open]

Faith is fundamental to the setting, not an invention of the Coalition. It concerns how much beings genuinely believe in, trust, revere, or devote themselves to someone or something. The Coalition has accumulated exceptional Faith influence because extraordinary numbers of beings believe in its members or their claimed authority.

Critical separation: Faith does not determine what supernatural ability emerges from a Rift, grant everyone the same spell set, or automatically set the damage/output of a Rift-derived power. The origin, form, and intrinsic function of an ability are distinct from the amount of Faith placed in its wielder.

Faith primarily shapes relationships of influence. In the Coalition's case, Faith makes believers susceptible to progressively deeper influence and soul manipulation. The original story also establishes that Faith sustains the Coalition members' physical health and strength; the exact mechanism is not yet defined and should not be conflated with upgrading every supernatural ability.

Baseline rules retained from the original story

  1. The Coalition can forcibly control only a small number of unwilling individuals; these include the Accord's leaders.
  2. The less Faith a soul has in Coalition members, the less those members can influence it through Faith.
  3. They can influence any number of souls that place sufficient Faith in them.
  4. They cannot force a person to genuinely believe more.
  5. At sufficiently high Faith, they can alter a soul, with corresponding changes to its body.
  6. Faith strengthens and sustains Coalition members, while consuming souls prolongs their lives. These are distinct benefits.

Proposed influence spectrum [Proposal, not numerical canon]

Relationship Possible effect of Coalition influence
No Faith No voluntary Faith channel; only the small, separate forced-control capacity applies
Trust Limited nudging of perceptions, moods, and decisions
Devotion Direct commands become possible
Absolute Faith Soul modification and bodily transformation become possible

Important distinction: ordinary trust is not necessarily obedience. The threshold for actual supernatural influence, whether Faith can empower any recipient, and whether it manifests differently among beings are still Open. No universal Faith-to-power conversion formula has been decided.

Why this is cosmic without being a dualistic pantheon

The Queen and Coalition are participants in a universe where Faith exists; they are not established as incarnations of opposite cosmic principles. Their contrast can be philosophical and biological rather than a predestined war between creation and destruction. The source of Faith itself is deliberately unresolved.

4. The Coalition (formerly X) [Established foundation]

The Coalition is a small group of individuals ruling its own founding world. They are the same race as their people but possess exceptional, witch-like abilities. Their number, titles, individual identities, and whether “Coalition” is their official public name remain Open.

Their real problem: an unsustainable soul economy

Faith makes them healthier, stronger, and extraordinarily difficult to assassinate. But it does not grant unlimited life. To extend their lifespans, they consume the souls of the faithful. A consumed soul is gone, and a soulless person cannot become a conventional Faith-based follower. Their own population cannot replace consumed souls quickly enough. They need followers from beyond their home world.

Why they engineered the war

The Coalition secretly controls the Accord's leaders, but cannot directly compel whole civilizations into genuine belief. It uses leaders, propaganda, economic pressure, and conflict to make life unbearable. It then offers itself as the only credible source of peace and stability. By manufacturing the need for salvation, it increases the number of people who freely place Faith in it.

Earth's central position amplifies this strategy: influence over its interworld institutions can shape the fate of every connected world without physically conquering each one.

The false utopia

From outside, the Coalition's home world appears prosperous, harmonious, and nearly free of conflict. The bodies left behind by consumed followers decompose into an extraordinary building material. Every building on the Coalition's world is made from dead followers. This disturbing truth is established in the original lore.

Proposals retained: citizens may honor being used to build the city; pristine districts may stand empty as population declines; the material may be exported as luxury stone to other worlds; records of vanished assassins may reveal the system's true cost.

What the Coalition believes [Working interpretation]

The Coalition may sincerely view its continued existence as essential to civilization. Its members can point to genuine peace and prosperity on their home world as justification for sacrifice. Their ideological contradiction is that they promise enduring security while permanently destroying the individuals whose souls sustain that security.

This possible self-justification does not replace their established material need to consume souls.

The truce [Original event; interpretation proposed]

The Accord's sudden agreement to fight the Queen's colony may be the first conspicuous clue that its leaders are being coordinated. Proposal: the Coalition brokers the truce and claims credit, increasing Faith while redirecting attention toward an enemy it cannot easily control.

5. The Queen and her colony [Established foundation]

The newly discovered world is a primitive, ant-colony-like civilization, distinct from the AI world.

  • Subjects: The Queen's insect-like subjects have no souls, preventing the Coalition's usual soul-based influence over them. She sends subjects to events and expeditions.
  • The Queen: She has a soul but has never come into direct contact with the Coalition.
  • Consumption and rebirth: She can consume a person, retain their soul and memories, and give birth to that person again.
  • Memory: She keeps the memories of those she consumes.
  • Perception: She can sense faithful souls being consumed by the Coalition in real time.
  • Civilization: She attempts to advance her colony; the world's growth can reflect player unlocks and achievements.

Why she opposes the Coalition [Working interpretation]

The Queen can sense the permanent disappearance of consumed souls. Unlike the Coalition, she retains souls and can restore their bearers to life. Her opposition can therefore stem from a real distinction between preservation and annihilation, as well as the immediate danger that the Coalition's expanding influence poses to her colony.

She need not be benevolent. Preserving a soul without its owner's consent may still be a form of possession. Her motive could include survival, preservation, curiosity, and attachment to the lives she holds; the relative importance of these motives remains Open.

Philosophical contrast [Working interpretation]

Question Queen Coalition
What happens to captured souls? Retained; rebirth is possible Consumed permanently
How is civilization sustained? Colony, retained memory, reproduction, advancement Faith, obedience, and consumption
Relationship to autonomy Can preserve someone without their consent Can command or reshape the sufficiently faithful
Vulnerability She has a soul and can potentially be targeted Their longevity depends on a continued soul supply
Moral tension Is preservation without consent salvation? Is imposed purpose worth destruction of the self?

These two powers are not confirmed cosmic opposites, and there is no established shared origin for their abilities.

Potential revelation [Proposal]

Accord propaganda says the Queen eats people. This is true. Later, Champions discover that she consumed them as part of their rescue and rebirth. The revelation complicates their allegiance rather than automatically turning her into a villain.

Proposal: the Queen learned about the Coalition's secret by consuming someone with direct knowledge. Proposal: every advance in her colony requires memories or expertise gained from a consumed individual, giving progression a moral cost.

6. Souls, death, and rebirth [Established rules and necessary boundaries]

Established

  • Souls are real and can be influenced, held, altered, or consumed under specific conditions.
  • The Coalition's consumption permanently removes a soul.
  • The Queen can retain consumed people's souls and memories and rebirth them.
  • The Champions' souls are linked together.
  • Proximity between linked Champions permits recovery from near death.
  • Going down weakens the connection.
  • When the entire party is downed, their bodies lose their connection to the Queen, and they return to her world through rebirth. A party wipe ends the run.

Working reconciliation

The word consume describes different outcomes for the two factions: the Queen ingests or takes in an individual while retaining the soul, whereas the Coalition destroys a soul as fuel. These are not identical processes merely because they share a verb.

The Queen's colony serves as the resurrection/progression hub. Earth serves as the political and geographic hub of the Rift network. They are different places with different functions. Since the Queen's world is a connected world, its known Rift route should also lead to Earth; how Champions travel between deployments and the Queen's world is not yet specified.

Not yet canon

We have not established whether the Queen stores the original soul inside herself continuously, whether reborn bodies are biologically identical, whether Champions consented, whether her custody makes souls immune to the Coalition, or whether Champions can eventually reclaim their souls. These questions affect major reveals and game systems and must not be silently assumed resolved.

7. Champions and their mission [Established direction]

The Champions come from worlds that were recently at war. They are not disposable consumables. Their linked souls allow them to keep fighting, with the Queen's rebirth ability providing the narrative basis for the roguelike run loop.

Their mission is to maintain peace without closing the Rifts. They cross from Earth into connected worlds to investigate and interrupt the causes of conflict, contain incursions, and prevent dangers from spreading back into Earth. Some threats may already have crossed over and require intervention at both ends.

Mission types [Working gameplay interpretation]

  • Intervention: Stop a conflict before it spills through a Rift.
  • Containment: Trace an emerging threat back to its source and prevent another incursion.
  • Stabilization: Address a political, technological, magical, or environmental crisis endangering the network.
  • Investigation: Expose misinformation, disappearances, transformed followers, or the Coalition's manipulation.

Not every threat needs to be a Coalition plot. Connected civilizations have their own genuine problems, histories, and competing interests.

The Oathbreaker [Existing character concept; integration proposed]

The Oathbreaker is a fallen warrior seeking to prove that his oath was not truly broken. His Codex contains souls that do not willingly serve him; he compels them to his advantage in pursuit of atonement. This is his established character premise from the broader project, not a claim made in the original story.md.

Proposed thematic integration: the Oathbreaker provides a personal-scale reflection of the Queen and Coalition. Each uses or controls souls while believing there is a justification. His arc can explore whether conviction grants moral legitimacy, without requiring him to become redeemed or to release the sword's souls. His specific homeland, connection to the Rift war, and ultimate fate remain Open.

Coalition's world False utopia built from consumed followers Established
Queen's world Insect-like colony and rebirth/progression hub Established
Magic world Fantasy civilization with magical systems Idea
AI world Futuristic civilization with sentient robots and AI Idea
Medieval world Civilization of gods, spirits, and older traditions Idea

Each world can have its own terrain, generation, encounters, enemies, cultures, political disputes, and cards. The Rift network gives these differences a shared geographic logic: all roads lead through Earth.

Open: whether sentient AIs have souls, and whether artificial belief can participate in Faith. This must be resolved before defining the AI world's relationship to the Coalition.

9. Narrative progression across runs [Proposal]

The game is a run-based strategy/card game, closer structurally to Slay the Spire 2 than to a continuous military campaign. Story information emerges across many independent runs.

Phase I — The Fractured Peace

Players encounter an unstable Earth, competing civilizations, and a newly declared truce. The Champions are recruited or redirected into interventions. The Coalition appears to support stability; the Queen appears threatening or incomprehensible.

Phase II — The Weight of Belief

Missions reveal that Faith can be exploited to influence people. Some enemies are genuinely devoted, others are coerced, and some crises have no connection to the Coalition. Contradictions appear between official broadcasts and events on the ground.

Phase III — The Cost of Rebirth

The Champions learn more about the Queen's consumption and the conditions of their survival. The player's reliance on rebirth becomes morally complicated. The Oathbreaker's relationship with his sword can echo this theme.

Phase IV — The Perfect World

The Champions reach the Coalition's utopia and discover its material and spiritual foundations. The final confrontation tests whether the system of Faith-based control can be broken without destroying the societies entangled with it.

This is a proposed discovery structure, not a finalized fixed four-act run structure. A story phase may span multiple runs and worlds.

10. How the story fits the game

World routing

Selecting a world means crossing its Earth-linked Rift and entering that world's unique generated maps and scenarios. The run-map structure and whether Earth itself is a playable map are Open.

The linked-soul mechanic

The team can recover from near death when its soul-link conditions are met. Downed allies weaken the link. A full wipe ends the run and returns the party to the Queen's colony. Exact hex distance, downed penalties, solo behavior, and multi-tile rules are Open.

Faith as strategic pressure [Proposal]

The Coalition may gain influence in worlds the player neglects. This could change enemies, events, or final encounters. Crucially, such influence should represent the spread of belief and political/spiritual control—not a generic multiplier that increases every Rift-derived power's damage.

Propaganda and discovery

Short Accord broadcasts present the official version of events. Some claims are lies; some are true but misleading. Discoveries during runs let players reinterpret previous messages. The Queen's consumption of people is an example of a disturbing truth that propaganda could exploit.

Persistent progression

The Queen's colony changes as players unlock content and achieve milestones. Its growth may also have narrative costs if advancement depends on memories of consumed people (Proposal).

11. Decisions and reconciliations made in this revision

  1. Rift geography updated: the previous general world-to-world Rift description is superseded by an Earth-centered convergence network.
  2. Earth's role added: Earth is the ordinary human world transformed by arrivals from connected civilizations.
  3. Champions' mission expanded: they preserve peace through intervention, not routine Rift closure.
  4. Faith elevated: Faith is fundamental and relational, rather than simply a resource invented by the villains.
  5. Power categories separated: Faith influence is not the intrinsic output or origin of Rift-derived supernatural abilities.
  6. Coalition terminology introduced: “Coalition” is used as a provisional name for original X; “Accord” remains the interworld diplomatic institution. The two must not be confused.
  7. Two hubs clarified: Earth is the geographic/political hub; the Queen's world is the resurrection/progression hub.
  8. Queen and Coalition contrast clarified: preservation versus permanent consumption is central, without treating either as a primordial cosmic deity.
  9. Unresolved questions preserved: no invented final ending, soul metaphysics, Coalition membership, or Rift creator has been declared canon.

12. Optional narrative developments [Proposals, not canon]

  • Coalition forced control may require direct contact, making the Queen's location a closely guarded secret.
  • The Coalition brokers the abrupt truce against the colony and benefits from the resulting surge of trust.
  • The Coalition deliberately times the truce because war deaths can waste souls that might otherwise become followers.
  • The Queen learned the Coalition's secret from someone she consumed.
  • Champions discover that the Queen consumed them during their rescue.
  • The Queen's progress depends on memories and expertise obtained through consumption.
  • Coalition citizens regard becoming building material as an honor; abandoned districts and exported stone reveal the cost of the utopia.
  • Strongly faithful enemies can be transformed into unusual combat forms.
  • Neglected worlds experience increasing Coalition influence.
  • The Coalition's world could be the final destination, with its vulnerability tied to Faith disrupted across the run.
  • Faith may affect all beings but manifest differently depending on the recipient; this was recommended in discussion but not explicitly selected.
  • The Queen may regard preservation as mercy, even while refusing to surrender control of the souls she holds.

13. Open questions — prioritized

Critical: rules of existence

  1. What is a soul? Can it exist without a body, and what exactly distinguishes the Queen's retention from Coalition consumption?
  2. What happens to a consumed person's body? Does Coalition consumption kill immediately? What biological process produces its building material?
  3. How does rebirth work? Does the Queen reconstruct bodies, birth new bodies, or perform another process? What persists across deaths?
  4. Who owns a reborn Champion's soul? Can Champions leave, refuse rebirth, reclaim their souls, or be consumed by the Coalition?
  5. How does Faith operate universally? Can anyone receive it, only certain beings harness it, or does it manifest differently by recipient?
  6. What counts as genuine belief? Does Faith require worship, trust, love, conviction, or recognition? Can belief in an institution reach an individual ruler?
  7. What is the relationship between Faith and souls? Are souls necessary to generate or receive Faith, and how does this affect soulless subjects and AI?

Critical: motivations and dramatic stakes

  1. Why does the Queen oppose the Coalition beyond protecting her colony? What does she want from the Champions?
  2. What do Coalition members individually believe, and do they disagree about their methods?
  3. Why were these particular Champions chosen, and what makes them able to resist when conventional armies fail?
  4. What is the personal price of serving the Queen, and when do Champions learn it?
  5. What would victory actually mean: killing Coalition members, ending consumption, breaking their monopoly on Faith, reforming the Accord, or something else?

Worldbuilding and chronology

  1. Why do all Rifts converge on Earth? Is this natural, engineered, or unknown even to the most powerful beings?
  2. When did the Rifts appear, and when did the war begin? Original suggestion: roughly 60 years and 12 years ago, respectively.
  3. Who governs Earth, and what legal authority do the Accord and Champions have?
  4. Can Rifts be closed? What happens to residents and trade when one is interrupted?
  5. How do Champions travel between Earth, the Queen's colony, and individual missions?
  6. What are the Coalition's and Queen's worlds called? What is the Coalition's official name and how many members exist?
  7. Do AIs have souls? Can artificial minds experience or direct Faith?
  8. How does the Queen communicate with Champions, and what do ordinary people know about her?

Gameplay implementation

  1. What is the exact linked-soul range in hexes, including multi-tile units?
  2. How much does a downed Champion weaken the link, and what happens in solo play?
  3. Do worlds have their own card affinities and distinct act roles?
  4. Which unlocks, memories, world states, and narrative discoveries persist across runs?
  5. How does Coalition Faith influence affect route choice without making players feel punished for exploring preferred worlds?
  6. How are Earth-side incidents represented in a run that primarily takes place beyond Rifts?

15. One-paragraph narrative pitch

When impossible Rifts turned Earth into the crossroads of many worlds, humanity and its new neighbors forged the Accord to survive together. Decades later, engineered wars have driven those civilizations toward a mysterious Coalition whose authority grows with the Faith placed in it—and whose rulers secretly consume devoted souls to sustain their lives. A newly discovered insect Queen, immune to the Coalition's ordinary reach through her soulless colony, rescues fallen Champions and grants them the power to return after death. Rather than sealing the Rifts and abandoning the people bound together by them, the Champions cross into troubled worlds to contain threats, expose the manufactured war, and defend a fragile peace—while slowly discovering the price of the Queen's gift and the true nature of the souls everyone claims to protect.
