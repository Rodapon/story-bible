THE RIFT CONVERGENCE

Story — Sanctum & Sovereignty Edition

Version: 2.1
Status: Consolidated story and Oathbreaker retcon
Terminology: The Stewards replace X / the Coalition. Faith is removed from this story's supernatural system.

Canon priority: The latest decisions about the Stewards, Sanctum, Sovereignty, Will, and the Oathbreaker supersede older Faith-based explanations. [Established] indicates specified canon; [Open] indicates mechanics or events not yet settled.

I. Core Story

Decades ago, Rifts began opening unpredictably between different worlds. These pathways connected civilizations that had previously been isolated, without any single world serving as the center of the network. The connected civilizations formed the Accord, initially sharing technology, magic, resources, and people. Tensions eventually escalated into a devastating interworld war.

The war was engineered. One of the Accord's founding worlds is ruled by the Stewards, a small group who secretly control the Accord's leaders. Their goal is to make other worlds desperate enough to willingly accept their rule and lower their souls' defenses, allowing the Stewards to siphon strength and sustain themselves by consuming souls.

A new Rift reveals the Queen's primitive, insectoid colony. Its soulless subjects resist the Stewards' ordinary soul-based influence. The Accord calls a truce against the colony; heroes from formerly hostile worlds meet on that front, and the Queen selects Champions to oppose the Stewards for reasons of her own.

Core factions

Faction / place Role
The Accord Interworld alliance, compromised through leaders controlled by the Stewards.
The Stewards Rulers who use asymmetrical Siphoning and soul Consumption to sustain power and lifespan.
The Queen's colony Soulless insectoid society; her scouts extend her perception across the world.
Champions Soul-bearing individuals selected by the Queen for Preservation and eventual Restoration.
Rifts Unpredictably appearing pathways connecting different worlds; no universal hub world.

II. Cosmic Principles — Souls, Sanctum, and Sovereignty

Term Definition
Soul An individual's spiritual identity and consciousness.
Sanctum The protective barrier surrounding a soul.
Sovereignty A soul's authority over itself, protected by its Sanctum.
Will Inward conviction and disciplined spiritual exertion; the Oathbreaker uses it to pressure another soul's Sanctum.
Siphoning Drawing strength through a connection between souls. For ordinary souls, the exchange is reciprocal.
Consumption Permanent destruction of a soul for the Stewards' continued lifespan.
Retention The Archivist's keeping of real, conscious souls without their capacity for bodily restoration.
Preservation The Queen's protection of a living soul's capacity for later restoration.
Restoration The Queen's return of an already-preserved soul to embodied life.

Sanctum rules [Established]

  • Every ordinary soul has a Sanctum that protects its Sovereignty.
  • To receive strength through ordinary Siphoning, a soul must lower its own Sanctum, exposing itself to reciprocal exchange.
  • Ordinary Siphoning is mutual and generally limited to a small number of close souls.
  • A lowered Sanctum can expose its owner to manipulation, loss of Sovereignty, and—if lowered sufficiently—alteration of the soul and body.
  • No individual can simply force someone to choose to lower their Sanctum.
  • Faith is not part of this cosmology. Earlier descriptions of Faith as the universal power or of Will as inward Faith are retired.

Will and Sovereignty [Established direction]

Will is the Oathbreaker's means of exerting spiritual pressure against a retained soul's Sanctum. When he successfully overcomes its protection, he can assert control over a measure of that soul's Sovereignty, compelling its skills or abilities through Ancestral Dominion.

This is not Siphoning: he does not gain the soul's strength through a mutually opened exchange, and he does not need that soul's consent. The soul remains an individual, conscious and potentially resistant; command does not imply loyalty, forgiveness, or belief.

[Open] The precise thresholds, duration, resistance, and consequences of forcing a retained soul's Sanctum are not yet defined. The established bloodline access limits still apply.

III. The Stewards — The Stripped

The Stewards are a small group from one founding world. They belong to the same race as their subjects but are born with complete souls without Sanctums; only their race can produce such individuals.

Aspect Rule
Innate difference Born without a Sanctum; also called the Stripped.
Siphoning Can draw from souls that lower their Sanctums without exposing themselves in return.
Force Can forcibly breach raised Sanctums, but only on a small scale; the Accord's leaders are among their controlled targets.
Control More freely lowered Sanctums allow greater influence over many followers.
Transformation Sufficiently lowered Sanctums permit changes to souls and, consequently, bodies.
Consumption Permanently destroys followers' souls to extend the Stewards' lives.
Political method Manufacture war and desperation to persuade populations to accept their rule voluntarily.
Homeworld An apparent utopia whose buildings are constructed from the remains of consumed followers.

The Stewards' central vulnerability is consent at scale: they cannot compel entire civilizations to lower their Sanctums, so they engineer circumstances in which people do so willingly. Their ability to forcibly breach a few raised Sanctums does not grant unlimited coercive reach.

IV. The Queen and Her Colony

The Queen is an ancient, soul-bearing insectoid entity whose innate abilities include creating soulless insectoid life, Preservation, and Restoration. Her colony functions through independent insect instincts and a biological sensory link to its creator.

Collective Sight — The Thousand Eyes

  • Passive awareness: Insects communicate primitive impressions of movement, threats, and unusual events.
  • Focused sight: The Queen can perceive through a chosen subject's senses.
  • Collective observation: Scouts survey distant regions and help identify possible Champions.
  • Limit: Scouts must physically reach a place; they do not automatically read souls or penetrate the Sovereign Veil.

Soul continuity

  • The Queen establishes Preservation while the subject is alive.
  • At death, a previously preserved soul retains the capacity for Restoration.
  • Souls already Retained by the Archivist cannot be retroactively preserved or restored.
  • Preservation does not automatically grant the Queen the subject's consent, loyalty, or Sovereignty.
  • The Queen's methods do not require physically eating subjects; older descriptions of her consuming bodies to preserve them are retired.

The Queen's motives remain opaque. Her preserved Champions can return after a party wipe, supporting the game's roguelike run structure.

V. The Ancient Kingdom — An Oathbreaker-Related History

The ancient human kingdom arose on an Earth-like planet. Long before its destruction, an anomalous encounter with the Archivist's old-man manifestation introduced its ruler to the principles of Will; the Archivist offered knowledge and direction rather than ruling the kingdom.

Over generations, the kingdom's scholars refined Will and developed the royal inheritance called Ancestral Dominion. This was an extraordinary capacity to assert authority over eligible retained souls, not a form of the Stewards' Siphoning.

The Convergence and genocide

The kingdom's planet was caught in a Rift Convergence event. One of the newly opened Rifts happened to connect it to the Stewards' world, exposing the kingdom to their civilization and, eventually, their ambitions.

The Stewards identified the kingdom's mastery of Will and soul Sovereignty as a threat to their system of control through lowered Sanctums. During the chaos, they launched a deliberate genocide against the kingdom and the people preserving its knowledge. The Sovereign Veil did not cause the attack.

[Open] The exact process by which the Stewards learned of the kingdom's techniques, and whether they initially posed as benefactors, has not been established.

The final contingency

In the kingdom's last hours, the reigning king, royal family, and surviving scholars enacted the unfinished Sovereign Veil. They sought to conceal the Archivist and the Ancestral Domain from the Stewards and save the spiritual inheritance of the royal line.

  • Concealment: Hides the Archivist, Ancestral Domain, and their spiritual connections from the Stewards.
  • Retention filter: Restricts future retention to the designated royal bloodline, reducing detectable spiritual activity.
  • Inheritance filter: Restricts the royal descendant's access to the authorized ancestral inheritance, not the Archivist's older Library.
  • Emergency: The kingdom's dying souls were retained during the final contingency, including some non-royal individuals; this does not automatically grant the Oathbreaker command over them.
  • Limitations: The Veil was untested, unfinished, lacked redundancy, and had no completed reversal spell.

The Queen is also unable to perceive the hidden Domain, an unintended consequence. Even if she could see it, the souls already under Retention lack the continuity needed for Restoration.

VI. The Archivist, the Library, and The Codex

Term Meaning
The Archivist Ancient, domain-bound entity whose innate ability is Retention.
The Library Its original pre-Veil collection of real, conscious, knowledgeable souls.
Ancestral Domain The concealed inheritance associated with the destroyed kingdom and its bloodline.
Sovereign Veil Artificial overlay limiting the Archivist's future retention and concealing the Domain.
The Codex Physical manifestation of the Archivist that requires a wielder to act.
Ancestral Dominion The Oathbreaker's inherited authority to command eligible ancestral souls.

The Archivist cannot directly conduct campaigns in the physical world or wield The Codex independently. Its old-man form influences through teaching; its sword form provides a physical conduit for an eligible wielder.

VII. The Oathbreaker — Story Synopsis

Generations after the ancient kingdom's destruction, a knight descended unknowingly from an illegitimate branch of its royal house. He swore to defend his hometown and family, devoting his life to the protection of those under his care.

During the unfolding catastrophe, the Queen selected the living knight and established a Preservation Condition on his soul. That earlier condition threatened the Veil's pending, death-triggered Retention of his royal bloodline soul. The Archivist responded by manifesting as The Codex, exposing the knight's dormant Ancestral Dominion.

Untrained and overwhelmed, the knight used his newly accessible power recklessly. His own actions caused the breach, destroying his hometown and killing his family; the precise action that triggered the catastrophe remains [Open]. He did not intend to betray his oath, but he was responsible for failing it.

His eligible relatives were retained by the Archivist. They remain conscious, and their silent acts of care—polishing his helmet, maintaining his sword, tending his gear—express familial devotion, not Faith or supernatural power transfer. Many other ancestral souls resist him or resent being compelled.

The knight became known as the Oathbreaker. He can remove his helmet, but refuses to reveal his face in another person's presence because of his shame. Through the Queen's Preservation and Restoration, he survives repeated deaths and journeys through the Rifts in pursuit of atonement.

How the Oathbreaker commands souls [New canon]

  1. Eligibility: The Sovereign Veil permits access only to souls within his authorized ancestral inheritance.
  2. Contact: The Codex provides the conduit through which the Oathbreaker reaches an eligible retained soul.
  3. Pressure: He focuses Will against that soul's Sanctum, meeting resistance from the soul's protection and resolve.
  4. Sovereignty: When he overcomes the Sanctum sufficiently, Ancestral Dominion asserts command over part of the soul's Sovereignty.
  5. Invocation: He compels an eligible skill, technique, or ability into action through the sword.
  6. Consequences: The soul remains conscious and may resent the coercion; the power does not manufacture loyalty or grant access to unrelated souls in the Library.

The central character conflict: The Oathbreaker can force an ancestral soul to act, but cannot make it forgive him. His mastery of Will may make him more capable, yet his redemption depends on what he chooses to do with that power.

IX. Canon Boundaries and Open Questions

Fixed boundaries

  • Stewards, not X or the Coalition, is the current antagonist name.
  • Faith is removed; explanations based on receiving belief from others no longer apply.
  • Sanctum protects Sovereignty; ordinary Siphoning is reciprocal, while the Stewards' is asymmetrical.
  • Will and Ancestral Dominion enable the Oathbreaker to compel eligible retained souls, without implying voluntary cooperation.
  • The Queen preserves living souls, and does not need to eat people to do so.
  • The Archivist's Library remains intact and inaccessible to the Oathbreaker's inherited Dominion.
  • Rifts open unpredictably between worlds, without an Earth-centered hub. The Ancient Kingdom is on an Earth-like planet, and a Rift to the Stewards' world appeared there during a convergence event.
  • The Oathbreaker caused the breach; the exact triggering act remains unsettled.

Open questions

  1. What determines a retained soul's Sanctum resistance, and how does the Oathbreaker's Will overcome it?
  2. Does compelled Sovereignty last only for one Invocation, or can it persist longer?
  3. Can an eligible soul voluntarily cooperate without its Sanctum being forced, and how would that differ mechanically?
  4. How did the Stewards first recognize the kingdom's Will as a threat?
  5. What exact action by the Oathbreaker caused the breach?
  6. What is the Queen's ultimate goal in selecting and restoring Champions?
  7. Can a lowered Sanctum rise again, and can forced intrusion cause lasting harm?

One-sentence premise: In a universe where the Stewards exploit lowered Sanctums to steal strength and Sovereignty, a disgraced knight wields Will against the Sanctums of his retained ancestors, seeking redemption for the catastrophe he himself caused.
