THE OATHBREAKER

Character Story — The Rift Convergence

Status: Working character canon, with unresolved details explicitly marked.
Scope: The Oathbreaker's origin, the Ancient Kingdom, the Soulbound Sword, and the souls of those he failed to protect.

Core tragedy: The Oathbreaker did not choose to destroy the people he swore to protect. A divine manifestation during a mortal crisis killed them. He survived, and carries the name of the oath he could not fulfill.

I. The Ancient Kingdom

Generations before the Oathbreaker's lifetime, an Ancient Kingdom existed on an Earth-like world. Its people developed a discipline of Will, rooted in conviction and spiritual self-determination. Long before the kingdom's destruction, the Archivist—an ancient, independent god—had appeared to an early king in the guise of an old man, bringing knowledge of Will through an anomalous precursor Rift.

When a later Rift connected the Ancient Kingdom's world to that of the Stewards, the Stewards discovered the kingdom's knowledge and perceived it as a threat. They launched a deliberate campaign of extermination. In the kingdom's final hours, its dying King appealed to the Archivist.

The Sovereign Veil

The King and the Archivist entered a divine bargain known as the Sovereign Veil. The Archivist promised sanctuary through Retention and concealment from the Stewards for the King's future blood descendants, provided their souls remained available to be Retained at death. The King pledged his descendants' souls to the Archivist's collection, although those future descendants had not consented to the bargain.

The Veil is not a mortal spell that controls a god. The Archivist conceals the collection by its own choice. Its motives are its own. Neither the King nor the player is owed an explanation for why the Archivist accepted the bargain, or what role the descendants might play in its designs.

The Oathbreaker descends from an illegitimate branch of this royal lineage. He does not initially know his ancestry. [Open] The exact route by which his family survived the genocide and passed into later generations has not been established.

II. The Knight Before the Oathbreaker

Generations after the kingdom's fall, the Oathbreaker lives as a knight, sworn to defend his hometown and its people. He has a family and a life defined by that duty. His ancestry is unknown to him; he is not consciously pursuing a royal inheritance.

His father incurs a crushing debt to an influential, high-class broker. The broker uses that debt to hold the town hostage, threatening the very people the knight has sworn to protect. During the ensuing conflict, a chain of events changes the knight's life forever.

[Open] The father's reasons for borrowing, the broker's identity and precise leverage, and the individual steps of the confrontation have not yet been decided. The broker's connection to the Stewards, if any, is likewise not established.

III. The Awakening

While the Oathbreaker is still alive, the insectoid Queen establishes Preservation upon his soul. This condition protects his capacity for future Restoration if he dies. It also takes priority over the Archivist's promised Retention at death.

The Archivist, in a state of divine slumber, manifests the Soulbound Sword in connection with the knight. Because the Archivist is slumbering, the manifestation occurs catastrophically: an explosion expands across the town and then violently collapses back upon itself.

The knight survives. The people he swore to protect are killed. Their souls are Retained by the Archivist and belong to the Ancestral Domain, remaining conscious rather than being destroyed. The Oathbreaker's survival is not proof that he deliberately caused the event. The catastrophe originates in the sword's manifestation during the Archivist's slumber, not in the knight forcibly using ancestral souls.

[Open] The precise trigger of the manifestation, the Queen's timing relative to the hostage confrontation, and why the Archivist was slumbering remain unexplained. The Archivist's deeper intentions are not required to be revealed.

IV. The Broken Oath

The knight had sworn to protect the town. He could not. His family and neighbors died while he lived. Although the disaster was not a deliberate betrayal or reckless misuse of a blessing, he bears its consequences as a personal failure.

He becomes known as the Oathbreaker—a name reflecting the oath left unfulfilled, not an oath he consciously renounced. He travels through the Rifts seeking atonement, while carrying the Soulbound Sword associated with the loss of his home.

The people he failed to protect are not erased. They remain themselves within the Ancestral Domain, capable of remembering him and judging him. Some may resent him; others may understand. None is required to forgive him.

V. The Archivist, the Library, and the Ancestral Domain

The Archivist's divine ability, Retention, preserves a soul's consciousness for knowledge. Retained souls preserve their identity, memory, and Sovereignty. They cannot be Restored to embodied life. What knowledge the Archivist seeks, and for what ultimate purpose, need not be explained.

The Archivist's collection is called the Library. It has no fixed appearance to visitors: each being who enters perceives it differently.

“The Library does not reveal what it is. It reveals what each visitor is capable of perceiving.”

To the Oathbreaker, the Library appears as a vast collection of books. The Ancestral Domain appears as one book in that collection, and each retained soul appears as a page. The townspeople who died in the Awakening are among those pages. These books and pages are perceptions, not physical objects into which the Archivist has transformed souls. The Stewards remain the only race established as able to manipulate souls into physical objects.

The Oathbreaker's connection permits him to reach the Ancestral Domain, not freely access every other collection in the Library.

VI. The Soulbound Sword and Domain Invocation

The Soulbound Sword is the Archivist's physical manifestation and a conduit through which the Oathbreaker can speak with retained dead. The Oathbreaker does not seize their souls, cross or weaken their Sanctums, or commandeer their Sovereignty.

His spiritual ability follows this sequence:

Will → Ancestral Domain → Domain Invocation → Ancestral Blessing → Manifestation

Through Will, the Oathbreaker reaches the Ancestral Domain. In Domain Invocation, he speaks a retained soul's name and asks for the power or skill that resides in them. The soul may voluntarily grant an Ancestral Blessing, which can then Manifest through the Oathbreaker or his sword. A soul may refuse; cooperation does not mean forgiveness.

This is not Siphoning, soul Consumption, or domination. The sword's role as a conduit is distinct from the destructive circumstances of its initial manifestation.

VII. Preservation and Retention — Condition Priority

  • Preservation (the Queen): Established on a living soul, preserving its capacity for Restoration after death.
  • Retention (the Archivist): Takes effect upon death, preserving the soul's consciousness for knowledge, but ending the possibility of Restoration.
  • Priority: A Preservation condition established during life takes precedence over Retention at death. A soul already Retained or Consumed cannot be Restored.
  • The Oathbreaker: The Queen's preexisting Preservation prevents his death from automatically adding him to the Ancestral Domain under the King's bargain.
  • The townspeople: Their souls were Retained after the catastrophe. This does not mean the King's bloodline bargain automatically applied to every inhabitant; their Retention is an event established in its own right.

Neither Preservation nor Retention requires taking away a soul's Sovereignty or damaging its Sanctum.

VIII. Appearance and Character

The Oathbreaker is a middle-aged knight with a handsome, weathered face, golden eyes, and a short white-grey buzz cut. He bears no facial scars. He keeps his face hidden behind his helmet in the presence of others, reflecting his shame and grief rather than physical disfigurement.

His family and the retained dead remain conscious individuals. Their relationship with him is not reduced to combat resources: their affection, anger, silence, and decisions are their own. Small acts of care—such as tending his equipment—can express attachment without becoming a supernatural power or requiring forgiveness.

IX. Established Truths and Remaining Questions

Established

  • The Ancient Kingdom was destroyed by the Stewards after its knowledge of Will was discovered.
  • The dying King made the Sovereign Veil bargain with the autonomous Archivist.
  • The Oathbreaker is an unwitting descendant of an illegitimate royal branch.
  • His father's debt to a powerful broker led to his town being held hostage.
  • During that conflict, the Soulbound Sword manifested catastrophically while the Archivist slumbered.
  • The town was devastated by an expanding explosion that collapsed inward; the knight survived and those he swore to protect died.
  • The dead townspeople were Retained as conscious souls within the Ancestral Domain.
  • The Queen's Preservation of the living Oathbreaker has priority over postmortem Retention.
  • The Library's books and pages are the Oathbreaker's subjective perception, not literal soul-objects.
  • Domain Invocation requests voluntary blessings; the Oathbreaker does not dominate souls.

Open — Do Not Treat as Decided

  • Why the father became indebted and how the broker acquired the power to hold the town hostage.
  • The precise chain of events during the hostage confrontation.
  • The exact circumstances that triggered the Soulbound Sword's manifestation.
  • The Archivist's reasons for slumbering, its bargain, or its interest in the descendants.
  • How the Oathbreaker first learns his royal ancestry and the names of the retained dead.
  • How the surviving royal branch escaped the Ancient Kingdom's destruction.

This character story follows the latest conversation canon. Older explanations involving Ancestral Dominion, Blood Tithe, Ancestral Compact, forced soul service, Faith-based power, or the Oathbreaker's reckless use of coerced ancestral souls are retired.
