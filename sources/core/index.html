Source: story (1).md. The text below is reproduced from the provided original story, including its own proposal and open-question labels. It may conflict with newer canon above.

Story

The overarching story and how it fits the game. It has three kinds of content:

  • Decided: everything not marked otherwise.
  • Proposal: an idea that hasn't been agreed yet.
  • Open questions: listed at the end.

Names are working names. The alternatives are listed under Names.

Premise

Decades ago, Rifts began opening between worlds. The worlds formed the Accord and peacefully shared technology, magic, resources and people. Years later, tension grew between them and became an all-out war.

The war was engineered. One of the Accord's founding worlds is ruled by the Stewards, a small group who have secretly controlled every Accord leader. Their aim is to make life so unbearable that the other worlds willingly come under their rule.

Then a new world was discovered: a primitive, insect-like colony that the Stewards have never touched. The Accord calls a truce to fight it. Heroes from the warring worlds meet on that front and are "saved" by the colony's queen, who sends them to take on the Stewards.

Souls

  • Sanctum: every soul has a sanctum, a barrier around it.

  • Sovereignty: a soul's rule over itself, which its sanctum guards. Anyone who tampers with a soul is taking some of its sovereignty.

  • Ruling: to rule another soul's sovereignty, your soul has to be stronger than theirs.

  • Siphoning: any soul can draw strength from another, but only both ways at once:

    • To receive, a soul has to lower its own sanctum.
    • A lowered sanctum lets the other soul draw from it too.

    So siphoning is always an exchange.

  • It doesn't scale: it's easy for all of a king's subjects to trust the king, but the king can't open himself to each of them in the same way. Ordinary siphoning stays between a few close souls.

  • The Stewards' race: only their race can produce a soul born without a sanctum. No other race can.

The Stewards

Stripped of their soul's sanctum, the Stewards must siphon other souls to survive, and every soul they take makes their own sovereignty harder to seize.

The Stewards are a few individuals who rule their own world. They're the same race as its people, but each was born without a sanctum.

They have a full soul with no barrier around it, so they can draw from others without opening anything in return. Their siphoning is one-way, and its scale has no limit. When other souls attach to theirs, their own personality doesn't change: they take strength, not identity.

  • Stewards: what they're publicly called.
  • Siphons: what they do.
  • The Stripped: what they are.

The Skein

The Stewards' followers are the Skein, and one follower is "one of the Skein". In public, a skein is a gathered whole, like a flock of geese or swans in flight. It's also a coil of spun wool, which hints at their fleece being spun up and taken.

Followers rise through four ranks, one for each stage of lowering their sanctum:

Rank Sanctum Meaning
Sheaf Raised Newly gathered in, still whole
Shorn Lowered Gave their fleece to the Stewards
Sheltered Mostly lowered Taken into the Stewards' care, an honoured inner circle
Stilled Fully lowered Fully at peace, with no colour of their own left

Each rank sounds like an honour in public. In truth, being Sheltered means being next in line to be consumed.

Rules of their power

  1. Force: they can force their way through a raised sanctum, but only for a few people. Those few are all the leaders of the Accord.
  2. Control: the less a soul lowers its sanctum to them, the less they can control it.
  3. Scale: they can control any number of souls whose sanctums are lowered enough.
  4. Choice: they can't force anyone to lower their sanctum.
  5. Transformation: a sanctum lowered far enough lets them modify the soul itself, which transforms the body.

Why no one can rule them

A Steward's sovereignty has no sanctum to guard it, so only the strength of their soul protects it.

  • When they're weak: a newborn Steward, or one who has grown weak, can be ruled by anyone with a stronger soul.
  • Once they're established: so many souls are attached to them that their soul's strength is unmatched, and no one is strong enough to rule them.

What they gain

  • Siphoned strength: what they draw through lowered sanctums makes their soul stronger and their body healthier and stronger. That's why they can't be assassinated, and why no one can rule them.
  • Souls give lifespan: the Stewards consume the souls of their followers to stay alive. A consumed soul is gone, and a person without a soul can't become a follower.

Why they need the war

The Stewards consume souls faster than their own world can replace them through births. Their world can't sustain them, so they need followers from other worlds.

Force can't get them there, because of rules 1 and 4. They use the leaders they control to drive the worlds to war, so that people choose the Stewards for themselves.

Their world

From outside it looks like a utopia. There's no conflict, and the Stewards have no reason to abuse their people because they already have nearly everything they want.

The bodies of the consumed decompose in a way that makes a perfect building material, so every building on the Stewards' world is made from dead followers.

Keeping the secret

The Stewards control what their followers and the Accord's leaders say, so information is easy to manipulate. Assassination attempts don't need a real cover-up: the Stewards only have to say security stopped them.

The new world

The new world is a primitive, ant-colony-like civilization of insect-like creatures. The AI world is a different world.

  • The subjects: they have no souls, so the Stewards have no hold on them. The queen sends them out to events.
  • The queen: she has a soul, but has never come into direct contact with the Stewards.
  • Consuming: she can consume someone and then give birth to them again, and she holds the souls she eats.
  • Memories: she keeps the memories of the souls she eats.
  • Her limit: she can only keep a certain number of souls and memories. That's why she isn't a dictator or a conqueror, and why she looks for talented people.
  • Sensing: she can notice the souls of the Stewards' followers being consumed, in real time.
  • The truce: humans at least have an aversion to insects, which helps make a truce against the colony believable.
  • Advancing: she tries to advance her own civilization, and players' unlocks, updates and achievements show up on her world.

The Errants (The Heros / Player characters)

The Errants aren't consumables. Their souls are linked together, and that link is what lets them fight on:

  • Staying together: while they're close together, they can recover from near death.
  • Going down: being near death weakens the connection.
  • All down: their bodies lose connection to the queen, and they respawn at her world.
  • A wipe ends the run: the errants return to the queen's world, and that rebirth is the story's reason for the run loop.

Gods

Gods embody concepts. They gain power through their concept and through offerings.

An offering is given outward, and the giver never lowers their sanctum to make it. Gods therefore never siphon and have nothing to do with sanctums, which keeps them clearly separate from the Stewards.

Visual direction

Across the whole game

  • Models carry their own colours: team colour isn't painted onto a model, and neither is affinity colour.
  • Team colour is an outline: each unit is outlined in its team's colour, so it reads against any world's palette.
  • Affinity colours stay on cards and the interface.
  • Few materials: most of a model is flat colour, with a material only where it earns its place.

The two central worlds

The Stewards' world should look beautiful and feel wrong, while the queen's colony should look frightening and feel honest. Players should be drawn to the utopia and put off by the hive at first, then feel the reverse once they learn the truth.

The Stewards' race

They're sheep–deer people with swan-like features: about as docile as a creature can look, which makes the Stewards a literal wolf in sheep's clothing. Swans add beauty and, in English at least, a sense of love.

  • Build: upright and slender, with a deer-like frame, hooves, large dark eyes and soft ears. It stays on a humanoid rig, with the ears, tail and horns as extra bones.
  • Fleece: modelled as chunky, sculpted clumps rather than realistic fur.
  • Horns: curled ram horns that grow in rings.
  • Horn rings are limited: a Steward has millions of souls attached, so the rings can't count souls. There are only ever a few.
  • The Stewards are born without fleece: their bodies lack fleece just as their souls lack a sanctum, which makes "the Stripped" a literal name as well.
  • Mantles of their followers' wool: each Steward wears a mantle made from the Skein's wool, and it covers their whole body. Their bare bodies are a reveal, saved for key story moments.
  • Only the Stewards wear cloaks: a cloak alone marks a Steward, even at a distance on the board.
  • Transforming drains colour: as a follower lowers their sanctum and is transformed, they lose their colour. This shows their sovereignty and individuality being taken away.

Swan features, without wings

The race is swan-like through its neck, markings and posture, and has no wings.

  • The neck: long, with a gentle S-shaped curve and an upright posture, like a swan on water. This is the strongest swan signal.
  • Face markings: a dark mask across the bridge of the nose and a small dark knob above the muzzle, like a mute swan's, on a sheep's face.
  • Feathers at the neck: wool on the body gives way to short, smooth feathers up the neck and around the head.
  • Mantles shaped like folded wings: the Stewards' mantles drape from the shoulders like folded wings. The only wings they have are clothing.
  • Movement: smooth and gliding, with the head held still while walking.

What the Skein wear

The Skein are mostly natural: their own fleece is their clothing. Each rank adds one small item, and the items change from personal to livestock-like until nothing is left.

  • Why no cloaks: a cloak would hide their fleece, and with it the colour loss that shows their sovereignty being taken. Natural fleece also keeps the saying clear: followers wear their own wool, while Stewards wear other people's.
  • Being Shorn never means being bare: shearing takes only part of the fleece, such as the shoulders or a ceremonial lock, and it grows back short. Bareness belongs to the Stewards alone.
Sanctum Rank Look Item
Raised Sheaf Full fleece in their own colours and markings, and an individual face A personal token, such as a woven sash, beads or a braided ribbon
Lowered Shorn Fleece cropped short where it was cut, colour fading, expressions softening A plain band marking the shearing
Mostly lowered Sheltered Pale and nearly identical, with calm, mask-like faces A thin band around the neck, or a small bell
Fully lowered Stilled No colour of their own left Nothing: no possessions, nothing of their own

The bell is an honour in public, a mark of being close to the Stewards. Privately, it's a livestock bell.

A concept sheet exists, made quickly with an image generator. It's a starting point, not a final design. Before it's used:

  • Mantles: they need to cover the Stewards' whole bodies, where the sheet shows bare torsos.
  • Horn rings: they should be far fewer.
  • Swan features: it doesn't have them yet.
  • Followers' cloaks: the sheet puts followers in cloaks and robes. They should be in natural fleece with rank items.
  • Labels: it calls the followers "the Fold", which is now the Skein.

How it fits the game

  • Runs: the structure is closer to Slay the Spire 2 than to Helldivers. Each run stands alone, and the story comes out across many runs.
  • The map: choosing where to go means choosing between worlds. Each world has its own terrain, generation and scenarios.
  • The queen's world: it shows what players have unlocked and achieved.
  • War news: short broadcasts give the Accord's version of events, which can be propaganda, alongside the truth.
  • The ending: left open for now.

What already exists

This is based on CLAUDE.md as of 2026-11-06.

  • Routes: a game moves through a route of stops, either generated in rows or fixed (RouteDefinition, routes.json). After a win, players vote on which exit to take next.
  • Encounters: each stop has its own map, map seed, activity and entities (EncounterDefinition, encounters.json). A fight's objectives say how it ends, and the default fight is lost when all players are down (players_downed).
  • Recovery: after a win, every downed unit a player controls gets back up (ReviveRules), and cards can revive units (ReviveEffect).
  • Available as data: maps, biomes, map features, scenarios, and pools filtered by affinity.
  • Available as rules: downed units, transforms, statuses, and unlocks shared across co-op players.
  • Staged, not yet in CLAUDE.md: a campaign of acts, each with its own route, is staged in changes 0127 to 0130. An act is the natural place for a world.

    • A world definition that groups encounters, map generation and an affinity.

Proposals

None of these are agreed yet.

Souls and the Stewards

  • Consumption is complete siphoning: consuming a soul means siphoning it until nothing is left.

  • Rule 1 needs contact: forced control only works through direct contact. If the Stewards reached the queen, they'd gain her subjects and every soul she holds, so her location has to stay secret.

  • Force uses the ruling rule: forcing through a raised sanctum works because a Steward's soul is stronger than the target's.

  • The heroes share two-way: their linked souls use ordinary two-way siphoning. That's why staying close lets them recover, and it's what the Stewards can never have.

  • Opening is a risk: a hero who betrays the others, or is taken by the Stewards, could do real harm because they're open to each other.

  • Young Stewards are ruled: a newborn Steward is weak, so the established Stewards rule them from birth. That keeps the group small and loyal, and stops rivals from growing.

  • A way to beat them: every sanctum raised against a Steward cuts off a soul and weakens them. Once a Steward's soul is weaker than someone else's, their sovereignty can be ruled. This makes "weaken, then rule or break" the path to taking one down.

  • Linked heroes add up: the heroes' combined strength could be what finally outmatches a weakened Steward.

  • Clearer sanctum levels:

    | How far the sanctum is lowered | What the Stewards can do | |---|---| | Not at all | Forced control, limited to a few people | | Partly | Nudge moods and beliefs | | Mostly | Directly command | | Fully | Reshape the soul and body |

Story

  • The truce is the first clue: every leader agrees within days after years of war. The Stewards also broker it and take the credit, so more people lower their sanctums to them.
  • The truce is well timed: war deaths waste souls the Stewards could have had, so they want it to end at the right moment.
  • The propaganda is true: the Accord says the queen eats people, and she does. The heroes learn that she ate them too.
  • Why the heroes matter: their souls are held by the queen, so the Stewards can't take or control them, while armies can be turned.
  • How she learned the truth: she consumed someone who knew, such as a puppet or a Steward's servant, and nobody believes her.
  • Her progress has a cost: each advance comes from the memories of someone she ate, so a forge might appear because she consumed a blacksmith.
  • Her progress stays: what she learns passes permanently to her brood, so the hub never goes backwards when she releases a soul.
  • The god of war: the war has made the god of war very powerful, so it has a reason to want the fighting to go on.

The Stewards' world

  • "Joining the city": the citizens know what the buildings are made of and treat it as an honour.
  • Empty districts: the population falls while the city grows, so there are whole districts of perfect, empty homes that visitors never see.
  • Exported stone: the Accord buys the material as a luxury stone, so the dead are in every world.
  • A trail of the missing: assassins sent by other worlds vanish, and players can find the records.
  • Mating for life: like swans, the Skein form lifelong pairs who share two-way. That love is exactly what the Stewards can never have.
  • "Stock": what the Stewards call their people in private. It sounds like "good stock" but means livestock.

Visuals

  • What the horn rings mark: something with only a few steps, such as age or rank. A Steward's horns could be so finely ringed that they look polished smooth, reading as "beyond counting" without showing a number.

  • The shearing festival: followers give their fleece to the Stewards in a festival, the public version of lowering a sanctum. It marks the step from Sheaf to Shorn.

  • The bell's sound: a quiet chime when Sheltered followers are nearby, both as atmosphere and as a cue for players.

  • The bare Steward is a black swan: without the mantle, a Steward looks lean and sharp, a little wolfish, while still clearly the same race. Their bare body is dark, like a black swan, which also means something rare and unexpected. The pale mantle against the dark body makes the reveal clear on screen.

  • The ugly duckling, reversed: young swans are grey and become pure white as adults. The Skein do the same as they're transformed, and their world calls it becoming beautiful, when in truth it's losing themselves.

  • Beautiful but dangerous: swans are fiercely territorial, so the race's elegance can have an edge underneath.

  • The final stage foreshadows the city: at the last stage, a follower's colourless wool and horn look like the ivory the city is built from.

  • Not too cute: eyes a little too still and smiles a little too wide, so players sense something is off.

  • The Stewards look like caretakers: plain robes, aprons, and tools such as crooks and pruning shears. They never wear crowns.

  • Telling worlds apart: each world should be recognisable by shape and contrast alone.

    | World | Shapes | Materials | Palette | |---|---|---|---| | The Stewards' world | Smooth curves, symmetry, buildings that look grown | Ivory and nacre, from the dead | Soft white, pale gold | | The queen's colony | Irregular tunnels and chambers | Mud, wax, resin, chitin | Dark amber, warm glowing greens | | Magic world | Spirals, floating stone, towers | Crystal, polished stone, runes | Jewel tones | | AI world | Hard angles, grids, modules | Dark metal, glass, lines of light | Graphite, with cyan and orange | | Gods world | Heavy blocks, tall pillars, banners | Weathered stone, wood, cloth | Earth tones, firelight |

  • Keep the Stewards' world and the AI world apart: the Stewards' world is soft, warm and organic, while the AI world is hard, dark and angular.

  • The queen's world shows what she ate: she starts with mud and wax, then builds copies of things from eaten memories, such as a forge in resin. Each copy is slightly wrong.

  • Showing souls:

    | Idea | How it could look | |---|---| | Sanctum | A steady ring of light in a neutral colour | | Lowered sanctum | The ring dims and opens | | Two-way siphoning | Threads braided between two souls, flowing both ways | | The Stewards siphoning | One-way streams pulling toward someone with no ring | | The heroes' link | Threads between nearby heroes that thin as they go down, which can also show recovery range in a fight | | Rifts | Tears showing another world's sky, kept different from soul effects |

Stewards, Siphons, Skein, Sanctum and Sovereignty all start with S, and Skein even sounds a little like "sheep", but only in English. Translators should pick each word by its meaning, not its sound.

Open questions

Story

  • What is the Stewards' race called, and how many Stewards are there?
  • What are the two timeline dates? One suggestion is about 60 years since the Rifts and 12 since the war began.
  • What do the Stewards' world and the queen's world call themselves?
  • What makes one soul stronger than another? Is it only the souls attached to it, or do age, will or training count too?
  • Can one Steward rule another, and is there a strongest Steward?
  • Do the souls the queen holds add to her strength?
  • Does a sanctum rise again once it's been lowered?
  • Does siphoning damage a sanctum for good?
  • What happens to a person after their soul is consumed: do they die at once, or live on for a while?
  • Do followers know that consumption is happening?
  • Can the queen release a soul to make room for another, and what happens to it?
  • Why does the queen care about the war, beyond survival?
  • How does the queen talk to the heroes?
  • Do the heroes ever get their souls back, and does she gain anything from holding them?
  • Do AIs have souls, and do they have sanctums? If they don't, the Stewards have no hold on the AI world either, and the queen's world is less unique.
  • Who are the heroes, and which worlds do they come from?
  • How does the story end? Can the Stewards be killed at all?

Visuals

  • What do the horn rings mark, if not souls?
  • Can followers be told apart from each other at each stage, and how much of that needs to show on the hex board?
  • What do the queen and her subjects look like?

Gameplay

  • What counts as "close together" for linked souls, in hexes, including for multi-tile units?
  • How much does each downed hero weaken the link, and how?
  • How do linked souls work for a solo player?
  • Does each world get its own affinity? This affects how cards are designed from now on.
  • What carries over between runs, and how much of the story depends on it?
  • Do the worlds keep a fixed role in a run, or can they appear at any stage?
